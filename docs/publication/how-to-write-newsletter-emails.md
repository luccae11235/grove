# How to write newsletter emails

Aflora emails do one job: give the reader a useful reason to open the week's
article. They introduce the learning question; they do not summarize or reteach
the article.

## Voice

- Curious, precise, and calm—not promotional or sensational.
- Intelligent but plain-spoken. Prefer concrete situations to technical setup.
- Confident about what the reader will gain, without promising mastery.

## Shape

1. **Subject:** pose a specific question or tension that the article resolves.
2. **Preheader:** complement the subject with the practical promise; do not
   repeat it.
3. **Opening:** present one small scene, surprising result, or prediction.
4. **Tension:** show why the obvious interpretation is incomplete.
5. **Invitation:** say what the reader will inspect, manipulate, or learn to
   recognize in the article.
6. **CTA:** use one concrete action tied to the learner's question.

## Restraint

- Keep the body to roughly 90–150 words and three or four short paragraphs.
- Introduce one question and one promised capability.
- Reveal enough to make the problem meaningful, but leave the explanation and
  resolution to the article.
- Use one CTA. Do not add secondary links or repeat the article's sections.
- Avoid generic openings, exaggerated claims, jargon lists, and urgency tricks.

## Final check

Before approval, ask: Is the question clear? Is the promised use concrete? Does
every sentence increase curiosity or clarify utility? Would the email still
make sense to someone who has not read the previous issue?
