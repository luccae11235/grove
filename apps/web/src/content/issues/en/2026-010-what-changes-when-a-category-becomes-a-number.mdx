Imagine a table of ten seed records. Each record names a species and says whether
the seed germinated. A tree can split a measurement at a threshold—but where
would it split a name?

Giving the species numbers seems simple. Yet the choice can change which groups
a tree can form in one question. And when an encoding uses past outcomes, we
need to check whose answers helped build each input.

The new Aflora article follows the same small example through category codes,
counts, target statistics, learned vectors, and native tree handling. Explore
the diagrams and three compact comparisons to see what each representation gives
the model—and when a training feature may reveal its own answer.
