Reinforcement learning introduces a long list of methods: Monte Carlo, temporal
difference learning, SARSA, Q-learning, actor-critic, and many more. Learned one
at a time, they can feel like separate ideas held together only by their names.

But each method begins with the same raw material: experience sampled while an
agent interacts with an environment.

Our new visual article follows that experience from state, action, reward, and
next state into improved behavior. Along the way, you will see the few choices
that distinguish major algorithm families: whether learning waits or
bootstraps, which policy generates the samples, what gets updated, and how the
learned quantity is represented.

The goal is not to memorize another taxonomy. It is to gain a compact mental
model you can use to make sense of an unfamiliar reinforcement learning
algorithm.
